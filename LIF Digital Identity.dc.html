01

Brand idea and emotional center

Someone thought of me.

People already think of other people when they discover something they love.

They send a link. They recommend a restaurant. They say: “You would love this.”

LIF does not invent that behavior. It gives it a better way to move. A person chooses someone specific, tells them why they came to mind and puts something toward whatever that person chooses. The receiver discovers who thought of them before seeing the monetary value, then chooses the exact product for themselves.

LIF turns a fleeting thought into a real human act.

THE TWO LAYERS

The human layer

A person thought of another person. They shared something they genuinely love. The receiver felt recognized rather than marketed to.

The infrastructure layer

LIF records the introduction and connects giver, receiver and merchant. It records Reveal, gratitude, recipient commerce and continuation.

The consumer should feel only the first. The infrastructure stays invisible in the emotional experience.

THE HUMAN BEHAVIOR UNDERNEATH LIF

Two audiences, one mechanism. Do not blend them into a single sentence on a screen.

Person, reason, funded value, anonymity, reveal, choice, gratitude, continuation.

Love It Forward is not a prettier gift card, a referral program, a loyalty tool or a SaaS platform. It is a contemporary consumer product distributed through brands. The identity has to carry the heart of the company and the precision of the product at once.

03

Product flow

One sequence, in this order. Every screen, email and example in this identity follows it. A step is never reordered for visual convenience.

  1. 01A giver thinks of a person.
  2. 02The giver explains why.
  3. 03The giver chooses an amount.
  4. 04The giver completes payment.
  5. 05The receiver receives an anonymous invitation.
  6. 06The receiver chooses to see who thought of them.
  7. 07The giver’s name and note are revealed before the amount.
  8. 08The receiver sees what is waiting for them.
  9. 09The receiver enters the brand and chooses for themselves.
  10. 10The receiver completes checkout with the funded value applied.
  11. 11Immediately after checkout, the receiver can send private gratitude to the giver.
  12. 12After gratitude and a clear pause, continuation becomes available.
  13. 13Continuation becomes real only when a new person, reason, amount and payment exist.
  14. 14Only after that new payment does the original giver learn that the love moved.

Gratitude follows checkout, not delivery.

14

Heart language

The heart is drawn by hand, not iconized. It appears only when a person sends love. Two families, used together, never resolved into a matched icon set.

FAMILY A — SCRIBBLED / FILLED

Dense, worked-over, filled with visible stroke direction. The white gaps left by the drawing are part of the shape, not a flaw to close.

FAMILY B — LOOSE BRUSH / OUTLINE

Single-pass, open, uneven weight along the stroke. Ends may overshoot where the hand kept moving.

THE FAMILY IS MIXED

Both families appear together, at different sizes and angles. No two hearts in a moment should be the same shape.

CHARACTER

Imperfect

Tactile

Human

Slightly raw

Textured

Graphic

Asymmetrical

Warm

Do not clean them into perfect vector shapes. Do not build an icon set of identical hearts. The hand has to stay visible in the mark.

COLOR

Poppy #CA0000

Pale pink

Poppy at 30%

Every heart is Poppy. The natural white gaps left by the drawing texture are good and should be kept. Softness never comes from dropping the whole heart to a pale pink or a low opacity — at the core visible moment the heart reads as full Poppy.

SCALE — THESE ARE NOT PARTICLES

Love Sent hearts are materially larger than ordinary UI decoration. At least one or two must create real visual impact.

Image language

Photography should show feeling, not things. It belongs to brand identity, editorial expression and storytelling — never inside transactional product UI, where the exception remains user-supplied gratitude media. The people shown here are brand imagery, not customers.

PRIMARY HUMAN IMAGE

The emotional payoff of Love It Forward: someone thought of me, I felt seen, that felt good. Large, close, uncontained — no card, no rounded corner, plenty of Air around it. Never inside product UI, never captioned as a customer.

SECONDARY HUMAN IMAGE

Connection, shared joy, ease. Used sparingly, and never in the same composition as the primary image. One human image per composition is enough.

ATMOSPHERE / LIGHT REFERENCE

A mood reference only. The lesson is light, air and warmth — not a subject LIF photographs.

18

The movement

Every Love It Forward matters on its own. When it continues, it becomes part of something bigger.

One person gives. Another person feels it.

A completely new act may begin for someone else. It is not a chain the product pulls. It is a decision someone makes, funded again, from the start.

Today the product is one person introducing another person to one brand. The longer-term vision is a consumer utility that makes generosity feel easy, contemporary and repeatable across brands.

INTERNAL ANALOGIES — FOUNDER, INVESTOR AND FUTURE VISION ONLY

Venmo for generosity.
GoFundMe for brands.

Strategic analogies for internal and investor conversations. They are not product definitions and never customer copy. They do not appear on a screen, in an email or in merchant material.

WHAT THIS NEVER BECOMES

Public generosity scores. Leaderboards. Streaks. Relationship data as advertising inventory. A social feed of who gave what to whom.

This identity is the visual system for LIF-owned email and web experiences. Apply it consistently before introducing additional expression.
